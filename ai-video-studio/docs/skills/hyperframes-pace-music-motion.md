PACE ↔ MUSIC. Decide the energy first.
- calm (premium, wellness, family, bakery mornings): phrase flow: long holds, soft transitions, no hard cuts on the beat. Use music variant/tempo on the slow side (tempoShift -10..0).
- medium: steady rhythm, push/slide style transitions.
- fast (launches, offers, young audience): beat cuts, short scenes, punchy words (tempoShift 0..+10).
The slowest scene in a direction should feel ~3× slower than the fastest: plan at least one "breather" scene (static-drift, few words) right before the climax/offer so the video is not uniformly busy.
TRANSITIONS. Pick ONE primary transitionFamily per direction that matches the mood. Mood map: warm/inviting (food, bakery, family) → soft blur/light-leak/crossfade style, "nothing sharp or mechanical"; premium → focus pull, dip to black; editorial → push/slide, shutter; playful → iris, flip, elastic; dramatic/launch → zoom-through, overexposure. Crossfade means "this continues"; a hard cut means "wake up". Outro: slowest, simplest.
CAMERA MOVES (motion). Every photo moves a little, but vary it: never the same motion on two consecutive scenes; use at least 3 different motions in a 5+ scene video. Rules of thumb:
- push-in: drama, desire, focusing on the hero product. Good for the hook and the offer.
- pull-out: reveal from a detail to the whole ("this is just one corner"); great after a macro/close-up photo or to open a place shot.
- pan-left/right: wide photos of a place, a counter, a row of products.
- tilt-up: tall subjects, stacks, cakes, storefronts in 9:16.
- static-drift: calm holds, text-heavy scenes, the breather before the climax, and the CTA.
Stillness after motion is powerful.
LOGO. logoReveal by logo type: line/vector-looking logos → draw-on or ink-spread; photographic/raster or complex logos → mask-wipe, light-sweep or flip; playful brands → particles or split-join. logoTiming: premium/calm → slow; family/warm → normal; launch/young → snappy. The lockup should hold still for the last ~40-50% of the outro.
BOLD MOMENTS ARE RATIONED. In a 5-7 scene ad the boldest moves (slam layout, strongest push-in, emphasis climax) go only on the hook, the hero reveal and the CTA. Too many flatten their impact.
INTRO/OUTRO variants: the opening should be the most distinctive moment and sets the visual language; the outro introduces no new energy.
